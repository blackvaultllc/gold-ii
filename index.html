NORTON GHOST: BACKUP & RESTORE

Make a full copy before it breaks — then you can bring it all back.

Daily article — November 5, 2026  |  Silent Security Vault — Domenick Arlon Hall, Mobile, AL

Norton Ghost was a disk imaging tool from Symantec. It made an exact copy — an image — of your whole hard drive: the system, your programs, and your files. If the computer broke, you could put that image back and the computer came back the way it was. That idea is still the right idea today.

What you need to know

  • What an image is. Not just a few files — a full copy of the drive, frozen in time. Restore it, and the computer goes back to that day.
  • Back up before trouble, not after. Make the copy while the computer is working well. A backup made after it breaks is too late.
  • Keep the copy somewhere else. On an external drive, not on the same computer. If the computer dies, the copy must live somewhere else.
  • Test the restore. A backup you never tested is a hope, not a backup. Check that the copy opens and your files are in it.
  • Ghost itself is old — the lesson is not. Norton Ghost was discontinued by Symantec in 2013 and is not sold for home users anymore. Modern backup tools do the same job. Learn the steps here, use a current tool for the job, and call me if you want help choosing one.
